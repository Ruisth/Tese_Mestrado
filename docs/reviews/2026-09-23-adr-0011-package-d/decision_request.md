*Recovered*, said of a delivery or an identity, here and in ADR 0011, means delivered to the controller after the kill or the outage and ending with an `accepted` line — or with a `duplicate` line in the N1 case, where the effect had already reached the twin. An identity that obtains only a `failed` line has an outcome line but is not recovered [ADR, after the options table].

**Recommended: option 5, under six conditions and one choice** [ADR, "The conditions option 5 carries"]. Why:

- Neither half works alone, and r02's guest-side evidence is consistent with both classes being real: about 1,810 identities left without an outcome line inside the controller (at least 1,809 by arithmetic, PROVED; at most 1,817 on recovered bytes, 1,985 without them; "received by the old process" CONSISTENT-WITH), the class that acknowledging after the outcome line addresses, and about 320 published while no subscription existed (CONSISTENT-WITH, on recovered bytes; 151 to 327 without them), the class that a persistent session addresses. The evidence proves that none of them obtained an outcome line, not how they divide between the two classes, and the recommendation does not depend on the recovered bytes [ADR, Decision; I2 §6, §9].

A line therefore proves at least one drop for its named client since its last CONNECT, never how many, and a later run on the same connection can drop without a new line: no line is not proof of zero drops. Declining C2 is therefore a knowing choice: the bounded implementation and the ACL stay unchanged, complete broker logs and per-identity reconciliation are kept, counter deltas and the disconnected-client pre-check are forgone, and a missing identity whose cause cannot be attributed stays indeterminate — never 'dropped by the broker' or 'lost in the controller' by inference. If that attribution is required, C2 is chosen prospectively with the minimum read grant (the two topics) and the affected authorisation test revised; either way `$SYS` is no per-session drain oracle.

## 1. What caused the backlog in `nominal-r01`

1. At 11.2 msg/s offered, the controller served fewer messages than arrived in every one of the 60 ten-second blocks of the 600 s window, so the number of messages inside it grew from 750 to 3,593 [D §3].
2. Nearly all of each message's latency was waiting behind earlier messages: queue wait is 99.96 % of the summed latency [D §4].
3. The single consumer handles one message at a time, and each measured message occupied it for 151.44 ms on average while messages were arriving (the served rate turned upside down), but what fills that time (the Ditto `PATCH`, the event-log write, waiting for an emulated CPU) cannot be separated, beyond the fact that for at least 63.3 % of it the controller was not on a CPU [D §5–§7].
4. Nothing was destroyed and the run stays failed: 3,794 identities were confirmed in time and 2,926 late, of which 85 were already late at the harness fetch and 2,841 had no outcome at that fetch and were accepted after the drain [D §2].
5. Under the same load the served rate moved between 4.60 and 8.55 msg/s in 60 s blocks, one failed load establishes no capacity, and what an empty start (no warm-up backlog) would have produced cannot be derived [D §1, §3.1, §9, §11.2].
