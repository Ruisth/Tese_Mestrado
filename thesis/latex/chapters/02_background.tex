\section{Edge Computing}
\label{sec:bg-edge}

\noindent Edge computing designates the paradigm in which computation and
storage are placed near the sources of data, at or close to the network edge,
rather than in centralised data centres. Shi et al.\ articulate its canonical
motivations: latency reduction, bandwidth economy, availability under
intermittent connectivity, and the ability to keep sensitive data close to its
origin \cite{shi2016edge}. For consumer households, these motivations
translate into a gateway-class device that mediates between local sensors and
any external services. Benchmarking edge systems raises specific
methodological questions --- workload representativeness, metric selection and
repetition discipline --- surveyed by Varghese et al.\ \cite{varghese2021survey},
which inform the experimental protocol of this work
(Section~\ref{sec:method-protocol}).

\section{Digital Twins}
\label{sec:bg-dt}

\noindent A digital twin is a synchronised, addressable digital counterpart of
a physical entity. Fuller et al.\ survey enabling technologies and open
research directions \cite{fuller2020digital}, while Minerva et al.\ analyse
architectural models for digital twins specifically in the \ac{IoT} context,
including the notion of the twin as a continuously updated state
representation exposed through uniform interfaces \cite{minerva2020digital}.
Most published deployments assume cloud hosting; a smaller body of work
studies twins on constrained edge infrastructure. Picone et al.\ design and
evaluate a modular edge digital-twin architecture on resource-limited
hardware, providing the closest methodological precedent for hosting and
measuring twins at the edge \cite{picone2023flexible}.

\section{The C2DTA Line of Work}
\label{sec:bg-c2dta}

\noindent Pinto et al.\ propose the Consumer-Controlled Digital Twin
Architecture, in which blockchain technology is used to give consumers control
over their smart devices' digital twins and data \cite{pinto2026c2dta}. The
architecture spans consumer-side gateways and distributed-ledger components.
Its published evaluation instantiates a single smartwatch profile at one
message per second on an x86 virtual machine. The present dissertation takes
the edge-gateway component of that vision and subjects it to a substantially
broader engineering and empirical treatment: a Yocto-built reproducible ARM64
platform, three concurrent device types, six workload scenarios including
fault injection and a 24-hour soak, and resource-consumption profiling. The
ledger, identity and marketplace aspects of C2DTA are context only and are not
implemented or evaluated here.

\section{Embedded Linux and the Yocto Project}
\label{sec:bg-yocto}

\noindent The Yocto Project is an open-source build framework for creating
custom Linux distributions for embedded targets. Its layer model, recipe
metadata and pinned-revision configuration make the resulting images
\emph{reproducible}: a documented checkout rebuilds the same system
\cite{yocto2024scarthgap}. This work uses the Scarthgap release line (5.0,
\ac{LTS} until April 2028) \cite{yocto2024scarthgap}, with all layer revisions
fixed in a \texttt{kas} manifest. The alternative approaches --- generic binary
distributions or manually configured systems --- were rejected because they
weaken the reproducibility claim at the platform's foundation.

\section{Containerisation at the Edge}
\label{sec:bg-containers}

\noindent Containers package services with their dependencies under \ac{OCI}
images and runtimes, enabling digest-pinned, architecture-specific deployment.
Felter et al.\ compare virtual machines and Linux containers on server-class
hardware, establishing the baseline expectation that container overhead is
modest relative to full virtualisation \cite{felter2015updated}. Morabito
evaluates container technologies specifically on \ac{IoT}-class edge devices,
concluding that containerisation is viable on constrained ARM hardware
\cite{morabito2017virtualization}. These results motivate the architectural
choice of running the digital-twin stack as containers on the gateway; the
resource measurements of Chapter~\ref{ch:evaluation} characterise the cost of
this choice for the specific stack used here.

\section{MQTT and Telemetry Ingestion}
\label{sec:bg-mqtt}

\noindent \ac{MQTT} is a lightweight publish/subscribe protocol standardised
by OASIS; version 5.0 is the current OASIS Standard \cite{oasis2019mqtt}. Its
\ac{QoS}~1 delivery mode (at-least-once) matches the reliability need of
telemetry ingestion, at the price of possible duplicates, which the receiving
side must detect --- a requirement that shapes the idempotent controller design
of Chapter~\ref{ch:architecture}. Koziolek et al.\ compare MQTT brokers for
distributed edge deployments and find substantial differences in throughput
and loss behaviour between implementations \cite{koziolek2020mqtt}; Mosquitto,
used here, is attractive for single-node edge deployment due to its small
footprint and operational simplicity.

\section{The Web of Things}
\label{sec:bg-wot}

\noindent The W3C \ac{WoT} \ac{TD} 1.1 Recommendation defines a standard,
machine-readable description format for the capabilities and interfaces of
things \cite{w3c2023wottd}. In this work each wearable type has a \ac{TD}
aligned with its \ac{JSON} Schema, providing an interoperable, standard
description of the twin's data model. Eclipse Ditto, the digital-twin
framework used here, documents native \ac{WoT} integration
\cite{eclipse2026ditto}.

\section{Eclipse Ditto}
\label{sec:bg-ditto}

\noindent Eclipse Ditto is an open-source framework for managing digital twins
as addressable resources: things with attributes and features, guarded by
policies, exposed through \ac{HTTP} and messaging \ac{API}s
\cite{eclipse2026ditto}. Ditto is self-hostable as a set of microservices,
which makes a minimal-core deployment possible on resource-limited hardware:
this work runs only the gateway, policies and things services with MongoDB
persistence, on version 3.9.4 (Chapter~\ref{ch:architecture}).

\section{Self-Sovereign Identity as Context}
\label{sec:bg-ssi}

\noindent The broader C2DTA vision includes decentralised identity and
verifiable interactions between consumer agents. In this dissertation, \ac{SSI}
appears exclusively as architectural context: the evaluated platform neither
implements nor depends on identity agents, ledgers or credentials, and no
statement in this document should be read as an \ac{SSI} or blockchain result.
The delimitation is deliberate: the research questions
(Section~\ref{sec:intro-rqs}) are answerable without identity components, and
the project plan classifies such components as strictly conditional,
non-essential scope.
